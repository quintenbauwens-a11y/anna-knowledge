What is bleed? And do I need it in my design?

Help centre article on Helloprint Ireland: https://www.helloprint.com/en-ie/help-centre/what-is-bleed-and-do-i-need-it-in-my-design

Category: Design definitions

Bleed is the part of your design that extends past the final size of your product, so colours and images still reach the edge after cutting. You need bleed whenever a colour, image or line in your design touches the edge.

Why bleed is needed

Cutting a stack of prints can shift slightly. Bleed makes sure that, after cutting, your background runs right to the edge instead of leaving a thin white edge.

How much bleed to add

Paper products need 3 mm bleed on every side, as set out in the file requirements for paper products. For other products, check the file requirements for that product before you upload. Keep text and logos inside the safety margin, because anything in the bleed is cut off.

If nothing in your design touches the edge

If your design has a white border or background and nothing reaches the edge, you don't need to extend a colour or image into the bleed. Keep text and logos inside the safety margin as usual.

What happens after you upload

Your file goes through our Basic design check and you get your digital proof. The lines and markings on your digital proof show where we cut, so you can see whether your bleed is right before you approve. If it isn't, replace your file and you get a new digital proof.

If your design reaches the edge but has no bleed, a thin white edge can show after cutting.
