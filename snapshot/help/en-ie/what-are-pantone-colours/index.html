What are Pantone colours?

Help centre article on Helloprint Ireland: https://www.helloprint.com/en-ie/help-centre/what-are-pantone-colours

Category: Design definitions

Pantone colours are standardised colours: the Pantone company has defined more than 2,100 colours, each with its own unique number, so the same number gives the same colour anywhere in the world. Pantone colours are also called PMS colours.

Why Pantone colours are used

Because each Pantone number always stands for exactly the same colour, Pantone colours are particularly well suited to corporate logos and house styles. A designer or printer anywhere can match your brand colour from its Pantone number alone.

Pantone colours and CMYK

A Pantone colour is one specific, ready-mixed colour. CMYK printing builds every colour from four inks: cyan, magenta, yellow and black. Not every Pantone colour can be reproduced exactly with these four inks. The difference between RGB and CMYK explains how CMYK colours are made.

Use Pantone colours in your design

  • Note the Pantone numbers of your brand colours, so you can check them against your printed products.
  • Set up your file as described in the file requirements for paper products.
  • Check the colours on your digital proof before you approve it. Colours on a screen always look slightly different from print: see why printed colours look different from your screen.
