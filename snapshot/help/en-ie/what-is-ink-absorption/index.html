What is ink absorption?

Help centre article on Helloprint Ireland: https://www.helloprint.com/en-ie/help-centre/what-is-ink-absorption

Category: Creating your design

Ink absorption is how well paper or another material takes in the ink printed on it. If your design uses more than 300% ink coverage in total, it is likely that not all of the ink is absorbed, and the print can smudge.

How ink coverage is measured

Ink coverage is the total of the percentages of each CMYK ink used in one spot of your design: cyan, magenta, yellow and black added together. Each ink can go up to 100%, so the total can go up to 400%. The material, for example paper, cardboard or metal, can only absorb a limited amount of that ink.

Example: a deep black

A black made of 91% cyan, 79% magenta, 62% yellow and 97% black adds up to 329% ink coverage. That is above 300%, so not all of the ink is absorbed and the black can smudge. Reduce the mix so the total stays at or below 300%.

Keep your ink coverage at or below 300%

  1. Open your design in your design program and check the CMYK values of your darkest colours.
  2. Add up the four percentages for each of those colours.
  3. Lower the cyan, magenta or yellow in any colour above 300% until the total is 300% or less.
  4. Save your design as a new file and upload it.

For large black areas, see the difference between rich black and 100% black. For the coverage limit for your product, see how much ink coverage to use.

What happens next

Once you upload your file, we check it and you get a digital proof to check before you approve it. The file requirements for paper products list everything else a print-ready file needs.

If you have already uploaded your file

You can replace your file until your order goes into production. See how to change your file after uploading. A new file means a new digital proof to approve.

Ink that isn't absorbed can smudge, and once your order is in production your file can't be changed.
