What is the difference between matte, gloss and uncoated paper?

Help centre article on Helloprint Ireland: https://www.helloprint.com/en-ie/help-centre/matte-vs-gloss-vs-uncoated-paper

Category: Before ordering

Matte and gloss paper are both coated: gloss has a shine that makes colours look bright, matte has no shine and is easy to read in any light. Uncoated paper has no coating, feels natural and absorbs ink, so colours look softer but it's easy to write on.

Gloss paper

  • Gloss paper is smooth and shiny.
  • Colours look bright and photos look sharp on gloss paper.
  • Gloss paper is harder to write on than uncoated paper.
  • Gloss paper suits designs with photos or bold colours, such as flyers and posters.

Matte paper

  • Matte paper is smooth with no shine.
  • Colours look slightly calmer on matte paper than on gloss, and text is easy to read.
  • Matte paper suits designs with a lot of text, such as brochures and leaflets.

Uncoated paper

  • Uncoated paper feels natural and slightly rough.
  • Colours look softer on uncoated paper, because the paper absorbs the ink.
  • Uncoated paper is easy to write on.
  • Uncoated paper suits products people write on, such as letterheads, notepads and forms.

If you're ordering flyers

For bright colours and sharp photos, gloss or matte paper gives the best result. You choose the paper for your flyers on the product page. Uncoated paper suits flyers that people need to write on, or when you want a natural look.

Matte or gloss lamination

Lamination is a thin film on top of the printed paper. It protects the print and gives a matte or gloss look. Where lamination is offered, you choose it on the product page.
