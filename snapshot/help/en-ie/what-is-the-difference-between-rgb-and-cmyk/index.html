What is the difference between RGB and CMYK?

Help centre article on Helloprint Ireland: https://www.helloprint.com/en-ie/help-centre/what-is-the-difference-between-rgb-and-cmyk

Category: Design definitions

RGB and CMYK are two colour modes: RGB mixes red, green and blue light to show colours on a screen, and CMYK mixes cyan, magenta, yellow and black ink to print them. Set up your design in CMYK, because we print in CMYK and bright RGB colours can look duller on paper.

RGB: colours made with light

Every pixel on a screen is made of red, green and blue light. Mixed at full strength, the three make white. Because RGB uses light, it can show brighter, more vivid colours than ink can print.

CMYK: colours made with ink

CMYK stands for cyan, magenta, yellow and black, the four inks used for printing. The printer applies the inks in layers, and together they create a full-colour image. Mixed together, the inks make a dark colour close to black.

What happens when RGB is converted to CMYK

CMYK can print fewer colours than RGB can show, so colours change when an RGB design is converted to CMYK. A bright green made in RGB, for example, is printed mostly with cyan and yellow ink and comes out darker and more muted. Mixing ink always makes a colour darker, while mixing light makes it lighter.

Set up your design in CMYK

  • Choose CMYK as the colour mode when you create a new document in your design software.
  • Convert photos and logos to CMYK before you place them, and check their colours after converting.
  • Export your file as a PDF in CMYK. See the file requirements for paper products for the other settings.
