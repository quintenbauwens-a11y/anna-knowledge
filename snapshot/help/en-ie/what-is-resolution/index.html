What is resolution?

Help centre article on Helloprint Ireland: https://www.helloprint.com/en-ie/help-centre/what-is-resolution

Category: Design definitions

Print resolution is the amount of detail in an image, measured in DPI (dots per inch): the number of ink dots printed along 2.54 cm. The more dots at the size you print, the sharper your print.

Pixels and DPI

A digital image is made of a fixed number of pixels. DPI says how closely those pixels are packed when the image is printed. The same image printed larger spreads the same pixels over more space, so its DPI goes down and the print looks less sharp.

Resolution for print

Print-ready files for paper products need 300 dpi at the final print size. See the file requirements for paper products for the full list.

Example: an image 3,000 pixels wide prints sharply up to 25.4 cm wide at 300 dpi.

Check the resolution of your image

  1. Open your image in your design program, for example Adobe Photoshop.
  2. Open the image size settings (in Photoshop: Image > Image Size) and switch resampling off.
  3. Set the width or height to your final print size in cm.

The resolution field now shows the DPI your image will print at.

If your image is below 300 dpi

Raising the DPI number in your program doesn't add detail, so the print stays blurry. Use the original or a larger version of the image, use a vector version of your logo (see raster vs vector), or print the image smaller.

Before you approve, read the pre-flight notes on your digital proof. If your image quality isn't good enough, you can still upload a better file until your order goes into production; a new file means a new digital proof.

An image below 300 dpi at its final size can print blurry or pixelated, because we print your file exactly as you approve it.
