Frequently Asked Questions for Table Talkers

What's the difference between the finishes?

Gloss lamination gives a shiny surface that deepens the colours of food and drink photos. Gloss and matt lamination both protect against stains and moisture; matt is non-reflective. Velvet lamination feels soft and very smooth, while no finishing keeps the natural paper look and takes a pen.

Is a table tent card the same as a table talker?

Yes: table tent card and table talker are two names for the same printed card, folded to stand on its own. You can choose a classic, with two panels that read from both sides of the table, or a triangle with three printed faces that reads from several directions.

What size are the cards for each shape?

A5, A6 or A7, in portrait or landscape, for the classic shape; flat sheets of 91 x 148 mm, 91 x 210 mm or 128 x 297 mm for the triangle. Triangle sizes refer to the sheet before you fold it.

What's the minimum number I can order?

50 table talkers is the minimum for both the classic and the triangle shape. Larger runs come in fixed quantities, and the delivery estimate appears as soon as you pick a shape, size and finish.

How do the cards fold into shape?

By hand, along folding creases pressed into each flat sheet, which takes a few seconds per card. The print sits on one side of the sheet, so once the card stands, every printed face points outward. The cards use 300 gsm silk with a matt look as the standard paper.

Add 3 mm bleed.

To allow leeway for cutting differences in production, always add a bleed of 3 mm around all edges of your design. Make sure colours, background pictures and layouts extend into the bleed area so as to avoid leaving white lines as a result of cutting tolerances.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

Keep all important text and images at least 4 mm away from the edge of the finished size.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

Save your files in an editable, print-ready file formats (PDF (1.4 or higher), TIFF, EPS, JPG). Deliver your files in one layer: the background layer.

Convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts.

Make sure you convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts when saving it to PDF.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.
