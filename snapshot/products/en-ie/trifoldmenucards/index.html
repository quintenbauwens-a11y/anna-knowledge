What Two Folds Do for a Tri-Fold Menu

Cover, Flap, Then the Full Spread

A tri-fold menu is folded twice, with both outer panels turned inwards, which gives you six panels. Guests meet the menu in stages. The cover comes first and a single flap follows before the full inside spread of three panels opens up. Each stage is a chance to show something different, and that sequence is what sets this fold apart. Folded, the menu is a slim card that sits neatly beside a place setting until guests are ready to order.

Lunch and Dinner Under One Cover

A hotel restaurant that serves both lunch and dinner can make good use of that sequence. The flap carries the lighter lunch dishes, so daytime guests have what they need as soon as they open the menu, while the three inside panels hold the full dinner card. One menu covers the whole day without mixing the two. In the evening, staff can guide dinner guests straight past the flap to the inside, while at lunch the flap may be all a guest needs to read.

Small Plates in Neat Columns

A tapas bar often lists many small dishes. Spread across three inside panels, they fall into neat columns such as cold plates and hot plates, so guests can build a selection to share. A vegetarian restaurant can use the flap to explain its approach or highlight seasonal produce before guests reach the dishes themselves. Its back panel can then carry the drinks and a short note on suppliers.

Tri-Fold or Zigzag?

Both folds give six panels, but they open in a different way. With Z fold menus, the sheet opens out in one pull and every panel is visible at once, which works for a takeaway menu. A tri-fold menu holds back the inside until guests open the flap, so you control what they read first. Frequently Asked Questions for Tri-Fold Menus

Which sizes can I order, and what is 1/3 A3?

At 297 x 140 mm, 1/3 A3 is the narrow choice; A4 (210 x 297 mm), A5 (148 x 210 mm), A6 (105 x 148 mm), DL (99 x 210 mm) and three square sizes are offered too. The sizes given are for the menu when folded. The narrow size gives tall panels, handy for a wine list.

When is one fold enough instead of two?

When your menu fits comfortably on four pages and needs no separate flap, half fold menus are the simpler option. Choose two folds when you want the extra panels or a section, such as lunch or specials, that guests see before the rest.

What paper weight should I choose for six panels?

On the table, a menu in 250 gsm or 300 gsm feels substantial while the folds still open easily. If you print large numbers or refresh the menu often, 130 gsm or 170 gsm keeps it lighter.

Can a menu with two folds be landscape or square?

Yes, landscape and square are offered next to portrait. In landscape each panel is wider, leaving room for a photo beside each course, while square panels make the opened menu look balanced and distinctive on the table.

File guidelines

Add 3 mm bleed.

To allow leeway for cutting differences in production, always add a bleed of 3 mm around all edges of your design. Make sure colours, background pictures and layouts extend into the bleed area so as to avoid leaving white lines as a result of cutting tolerances.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

Keep all important text and images at least 4 mm away from the edge of the finished size.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

Save your files in an editable, print-ready file formats (PDF (1.4 or higher), TIFF, EPS, JPG). Deliver your files in one layer: the background layer.

Convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts.

Make sure you convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts when saving it to PDF.

Fonts should be at least 6pt.

Fonts should be at least 6 pt.

Use the right values for full colour black.

Use Rich Black (C:63% M:52% Y:51% K:100%) for larger areas and text to get a deep black colour. Do not use this value for text smaller than 15 pt or plain text. This can only be used in full colour printing. For designing black smaller texts use 100% black.

Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Force Colour Images to CMYK with a 260% ink limit (in Photoshop > Convert Colour Profile > Photoshop 5 Default CMYK). Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure no elements in your artwork are set on overprint unless you want this effect in your design. You can read more about overprint in our Help Centre.
