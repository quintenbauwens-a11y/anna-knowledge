What the A2 Poster Size Is Good At

With 420 x 594 mm to work with, the A2 poster size has enough presence for a room but rarely crowds a wall, which is why it turns up in so many places, like a pharmacy window or the noticeboard at the GAA clubhouse. The format is also easy to plan around: it fits most frames and boards made for A2, and the same design works upright or on its side.

Everyday Jobs for an A2

Think about where your poster will hang and who will read it. A hair salon might use an A2 for its price list by the front desk, a guesthouse for a walking map in the hall. Each of those messages needs more room than a flyer can give, yet none of them calls for an entire wall. A2 sits in that middle ground. An A2 is also easy to change when the season or the offer moves on: one person can take the old poster down and put the new one up in a couple of minutes, so the wall never looks out of date.

Picking Paper for Everyday Use

Most choices come down to two questions: is your design photo-led or text-led, and how long will it stay up? Gloss makes images vivid, silk keeps words clear with less glare, and the 250 gsm papers are thick and card-like for a poster that has to last.

Moving Between A3, A2 and A1

An A2 sheet sits one size above an A3 poster and one below A1, so it gives you more presence than a small notice without needing a big wall. Sitting between the two is the whole point, and when you need more room, the A1 size doubles the space. All three share the same proportions, so a design made for one moves to another without being cropped or squashed. Plenty of businesses start with an A2 for the main wall and add smaller copies for doors and counters, keeping one look across the whole premises.

Frequently Asked Questions for A2 Posters

What size is an A2 poster?

420 x 594 mm. That's the A2 poster size this page prints, in portrait or landscape, so you can design at full size from day one and skip any resizing later. In landscape it is 594 mm wide and 420 mm tall.

Can I turn an A4 notice into an A2 poster?

Yes, and nothing is lost at the edges, since every A size shares the same proportions and A2 is four times the area of A4. Use the extra space for larger type and a bigger image rather than adding more text.

Which A2 paper lasts longest on display?

The 250 gsm papers. 250 gsm offset is sturdy and card-like, made for posters that stay on display, and 250 gsm gloss is non-bending and made to last longer. Both are a sound choice for a price list or a map that is not changed often.

Which paper should I pick for a text-heavy A2 poster?

Silk or offset. 135 gsm silk and 170 gsm silk keep colours clear with less reflection, so text is easy to read, while 250 gsm offset and 120 gsm offset give an uncoated, naturally matt surface. Matt lamination is another option for text-heavy designs.

Can I get double-sided A2 posters?

Yes, you can print A2 posters double-sided, with your design on both the front and the back. That works well for an A2 hung in a shop window or a glass door, where people walk past on both sides and see a finished design either way.

What does gloss lamination add to an A2 poster?

Gloss lamination adds a thin glossy film that deepens colours and shields the print from moisture and tears. It's recommended for photos, so it's a good match for an image-led A2 print that has to look its best in a busy hallway.

Can I print a map or timetable on A2?

Yes, A2 has room for a clear map or a full week's timetable that people can read while standing in front of it. Landscape often works well for a map and portrait for a list of times, and silk paper keeps small labels readable with less reflection.

Should I choose A2 or A1?

Choose A2 if the poster goes on a door, a window or an average wall, and A1 if it needs more room for detail or has a bigger space to fill. A1 doubles the area of A2, and designs move between the two without cropping.

Is one A2 poster the minimum order?

Yes, A2 poster printing starts at one poster. Printing one first is a handy way to see how the size sits on the wall of a guesthouse breakfast room or the side of a market stall before more copies go up elsewhere.

File guidelines

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

If you already have a design, no worries; we accept other file formats, including JPEG, PNG, or EPS.

For photographic content, high-resolution JPEGs are recommended, preferably without compression. For graphics or text-based content, we recommend print-ready, vector-based PDFs. For content that is a combination of graphics and photography, we suggest using print-ready PDFs to ensure the best quality.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.
