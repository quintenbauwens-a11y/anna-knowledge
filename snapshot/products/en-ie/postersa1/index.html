What an A1 Poster Does Well

A1 is the size people actually stop and read. On a wall, a 594 x 841 mm sheet is noticed from the doorway, but most of its work happens up close, when someone steps in for the details. That makes an A1 the natural size for anything that explains rather than announces, like a Transition Year project or a new service in a shop.

Room for the Detail

Compared with A2 posters, you get twice the space for photos, charts and a few short paragraphs, without the sheer scale of an A0 poster. A sales counter, a waiting room or the corridor outside a lecture hall are typical spots: places where people pause for a minute and have time to take in more than a slogan. The same sheet can stand on an easel at an open evening and then go up in a corridor for the rest of the term.

Laying Out an A1

Most good A1 posters follow a simple order. One title that tells people what the poster is about, a main image or chart that does the explaining, and short blocks of text around it, each with a single point. Upright is the classic poster shape, but turning the sheet on its side gives you a wide strip for a timeline, a process or a row of products. Leave some white space between the blocks, because a crowded A1 is harder to read than a half-empty one.

Paper That Helps People Read

Up close, glare gets in the way. Silk keeps colours clear with little reflection, while gloss gives photos more vivid colour when the image matters most. For a display that stays up all term, the thick 250 gsm gloss or silk sheets are sturdy and do not bend, so the poster still looks neat at the end of it. A firmer sheet also helps a poster of this size hang neatly on a wall or board, and if people are meant to add notes or names to it, an uncoated offset paper takes a pen without fuss.

Frequently Asked Questions for A1 Posters

How big is A1 size?

Exactly 594 x 841 mm, half an A0 and double an A2. That's the only size this page prints, and you can hang your poster upright or turn it on its side for a landscape layout, so set up the artwork at full size from the beginning.

Does A1 work for a product display in a shop?

Yes, an A1 has room for a large product photo with a few lines of detail beside it, for a display people look at from close by. 170 gsm gloss makes the colours vivid on a firmer sheet that holds its shape.

Can I print on both sides of an A1 poster?

Yes, double-sided printing is offered, so the front and back are both printed. That's useful for an A1 poster hanging in a window or a glass door, where people see it from both sides; for a wall, single-sided is the more affordable option.

Which A1 paper works best for photos?

190 gsm photo paper: smooth and coated, made specifically so pictures come out sharp and detailed, with deep colours. If you prefer a glossy look on another paper, gloss lamination deepens colours too and is recommended for photos.

Should I laminate an A1 poster people read up close?

For a text-heavy A1, yes: matt lamination is made for designs with a lot of words, and its thin, non-reflective film keeps them easy to read while protecting the sheet from moisture and tearing. Leave it off if people need to write on the poster.

Is there an A1 paper I can write on?

Yes, if you choose 120 gsm offset with no finishing. The uncoated paper looks naturally matt and is easy to write on, so an A1 poster can double as a sign-up sheet or a team planner that everyone adds to.

Should I go for A1 or A0?

Go for A1 if people will read your poster from close by, and A0 if it mainly has to be seen from a distance. A0 is exactly twice the size of A1 with the same proportions, so you can move your design up later without cropping.

Can I order just one A1 poster?

Yes, a single A1 poster is fine. A project poster for a presentation evening or one display for a reception desk can be printed just for that purpose, and you can order more copies of the same design later on.

File guidelines

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

You can download our guides and templates in the template section. Remember to delete the template layers before exporting your design to PDF.

If you already have a design, no worries; we accept other file formats, including JPEG, PNG, or EPS.

For photographic content, high-resolution JPEGs are recommended, preferably without compression. For graphics or text-based content, we recommend print-ready, vector-based PDFs. For content that is a combination of graphics and photography, we suggest using print-ready PDFs to ensure the best quality.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Force Colour Images to CMYK with a 260% ink limit (in Photoshop > Convert Colour Profile > Photoshop 5 Default CMYK). Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Using a border/frame is not recommended

It is recommended to count with a margin of error when stacks of copies are trimmed during the production process. This slight cutting deviation is usually not noticeable, nevertheless, we recommend avoiding adding borders smaller than 20 mm in your design for any product smaller than an A4 (210 x 297 mm).
