Branding Crowd Control Barriers at Irish Events

From Parade Routes to Regattas

Fence banners turn the crowd control barriers at your event into space for your club, committee or sponsors. Typical Irish uses:

  • the barriers along a St Patrick's Day parade route
  • the spectator area at a county show or a local regatta
  • the start and finish area of a parish 5K or fun run
  • queue lines at a summer festival or a ploughing match

Wording in Irish and English

If your event is run with a county council or a GAA club, you may want the wording in Irish as well as English. Plan both lines from the start and give them the same weight in the layout, so neither looks like an afterthought, and keep sponsor logos clear of the text on your fence banners. Ask someone fluent, such as your club's Irish language officer, to check the Irish wording before you order.

Frequently Asked Questions About Fence Banners

What sizes can I get?

Pick from 208 x 174 cm, 208 x 82 cm, 215 x 73 cm or 240 x 155 cm. Crowd control barriers in Ireland are usually about 2.3 m long and 1.1 m high (some hire models are closer to 2.4 x 1.2 m), so measure yours before you choose.

Can I get tie wraps with my order?

Yes, you can add black tie wraps when you configure your banner, or choose banner only if you already have fixings from an earlier event. Adding the tie wraps saves you sourcing fixings separately before match day.

PVC or PVC-free polyester: which suits me?

Standard PVC (510 gsm) suits banners left out in the rain at matches and festivals, because it is sturdy, waterproof and reusable. PVC-free polyester (225 gsm) weighs less and folds flat into a kit bag between fixtures, and its water-based pigment ink gives the brightest colours.

What if a stadium or organiser asks for fire-retardant material?

Bring standard PVC (510 gsm) whenever a venue or safety officer sets that rule, because PVC is the fire-retardant material of the two on offer. PVC-free polyester (225 gsm) has not been certified for it and fits open-air club days or fun runs where nobody asks.

Is the barrier part of the order?

No, you order the fence banners only: barriers and fence frames are not included. Quantities start at one, so a small club can brand a single barrier on a GAA match day, and a county final or festival can kit out every barrier in the ground.

Can I use these banners on Heras site fencing?

No, these banners are made for crowd barriers at matches and events, in four sizes. Heras fencing, whether it rings a building site or a festival perimeter, takes the single 338 x 174 cm size instead, sold as Heras fence banners.

File guidelines

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

You can download our guides and templates in the template section. Remember to delete the template layers before exporting your design to PDF.

If you already have a design, no worries; we accept other file formats, including JPEG, PNG, or EPS.

For photographic content, high-resolution JPEGs are recommended, preferably without compression. For graphics or text-based content, we recommend print-ready, vector-based PDFs. For content that is a combination of graphics and photography, we suggest using print-ready PDFs to ensure the best quality.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 150 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 150 dpi.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 5 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 50 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
