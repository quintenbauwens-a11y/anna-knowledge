Spiral Menu Cards for When the Menu Keeps Growing

Pages That Turn, Sections That Stay Separate

Spiral menu cards are made of loose pages bound along one edge with a metal wire-o spiral. Guests turn the pages like a booklet, and every section of the menu can have its own page. Where a folded menu runs out of room, a bound menu simply gets more pages, so the layout does not have to cram dishes into small type. Guests know how to read a bound menu without any explanation, just as they would a book.

A Grand Café Menu Without Compromise

A grand café often serves food from morning until late, with cakes in between. Bound together, those parts of the menu stay in order and easy to find, and guests can leaf to the section that matches the time of day. Staff can hand over one menu instead of several separate cards. A heading at the top of each page makes it even easier to find the right section.

Cocktail Lists and Pancake Menus

A cocktail bar can give its signature drinks, classics and alcohol-free mixes a page each, with space for a short description or photo of every glass. Because the spiral lets the menu lie open, guests can keep it at the page they are reading while they decide on a round.

A pancake house has a similar challenge with many sweet and savoury toppings. Spread over several pages, each combination gets room of its own. Children can browse the pictures while parents choose.

Spiral or Folded?

For a short menu, half fold menus with a single fold do the job with less to design. Pick spiral binding when the menu needs several sections or keeps growing with the seasons, as new pages can simply be added at the next print. A bound menu also leaves space for the story of your venue or a page about your suppliers, which a folded menu rarely has room for. The menu cards overview compares all formats.

Frequently Asked Questions for Spiral Menu Cards

Which covers can I choose?

Four cover options are offered. A selfcover uses the same paper as the inside pages, a medium cover is 170 gsm, a thick cover 250 gsm and an extra thick cover 300 gsm. The thicker the cover, the firmer the menu feels when guests pick it up and open it.

What sizes are available with spiral binding?

A4, A5 and A6 are offered in portrait and in a landscape version. A large square and a medium square are offered as well. Landscape and square formats give wide spreads, handy when a drinks list needs space for names and prices on one line. You also choose the number of pages, anywhere from 8 to 64.

What paper are the inside pages printed on?

For the inside pages you pick woodfree offset in 90 or 170 gsm, or gloss coated or silk coated paper in 130 or 170 gsm. Coated papers show photos smoothly; uncoated woodfree offset can be written on.

File guidelines

Add 3 mm bleed.

To allow leeway for cutting differences in production, always add a bleed of 3 mm around all edges of your design. Make sure colours, background pictures and layouts extend into the bleed area so as to avoid leaving white lines as a result of cutting tolerances.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

Keep all important text and images at least 4 mm away from the edge of the finished size.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Save the file for booklets as one PDF document with single pages.

Save the file for booklets not as spreads (two pages facing each other), but as PDF document with single pages, starting with the cover page (NOTE: saving as a multi-page PDF means 1 PDF file with multiple pages inside). Delete empty unnecessary pages from the file with the final save. Desired blank pages have to stay in the file.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts.

Make sure you convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts when saving it to PDF.

Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Force Colour Images to CMYK with a 260% ink limit (in Photoshop > Convert Colour Profile > Photoshop 5 Default CMYK). Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Fonts should be at least 6pt.

Fonts should be at least 6 pt.

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

Save your files in an editable, print-ready file formats (PDF (1.4 or higher), TIFF, EPS, JPG). Deliver your files in one layer: the background layer.

Use the right values for full colour black.

Use Rich Black (C:63% M:52% Y:51% K:100%) for larger areas and text to get a deep black colour. Do not use this value for text smaller than 15 pt or plain text. This can only be used in full colour printing. For designing black smaller texts use 100% black.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure no elements in your artwork are set on overprint unless you want this effect in your design. You can read more about overprint in our Help Centre.
