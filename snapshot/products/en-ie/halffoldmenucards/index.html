Why Half Fold Menus Feel Like a Proper Restaurant Menu

Open Once, See the Whole Meal

A half fold menu is a single sheet folded once in the middle. The result is four pages: a front cover and a back with two inside pages between them. Guests open it a single time and the full choice is laid out in front of them. That one movement is familiar to anyone who has eaten out, which is why the format reads as a classic restaurant menu rather than a handout.

Four Pages for a Steakhouse or Family Restaurant

Four pages work particularly well for a small to medium menu. A steakhouse can list its cuts and sauces across the inside spread and keep sides, desserts and wines for the back, so guests can compare a sirloin with a ribeye on facing pages.

A family restaurant gets the same clarity with a different emphasis. Mains for adults can fill one inside page and a short children's section the other, with drinks on the back. Desserts can share the back with the drinks, so the inside stays free for the main meal. Nobody needs to search, and parents can see everything while keeping an eye on the table.

The Cover as Your Welcome

The cover is the page guests hold first, so it carries more weight than its size suggests. A tea room might put an illustration of its shopfront there, or a photo of a cake stand, before guests open the menu to the teas and scones inside. A thoughtful cover makes the menu feel like part of the visit rather than a price list.

One Fold, Two Folds or None

If your list is short and changes often, flat menus on a single card are quicker to update. If you need a flap for a set menu or more panels for a longer list, tri-fold menus add a second fold. Every format is shown together in our menu cards range. Half fold menus sit in the middle: enough room for a proper menu, still simple to design.

Frequently Asked Questions for Half Fold Menus

Which shapes can I choose for a menu with one fold?

Portrait, landscape and square are all offered. Portrait is the familiar book-style menu with tall pages for a list of dishes. Landscape opens wide and leaves room for two columns of prices. Square gives a modern look that stands apart from standard A-size menus.

Glossy, matte or uncoated paper: which should I choose?

Glossy and matte papers are coated and give printed photos a smooth finish; glossy shines, matte stays subdued. Uncoated paper can be written on, which is handy if staff add a dish or a price by hand. The range also includes recycled and natural-look papers.

How heavy should the cover be?

Pick 300 gsm, 350 gsm or 400 gsm when guests should feel a solid cover in their hands. Choose the lighter 170 gsm or 250 gsm for a menu you update every few weeks.

What sizes are available?

A4 (210 x 297 mm) is the largest option, followed by A5 (148 x 210 mm) and A6 (105 x 148 mm). Each size is the folded size of the menu. There are also two slim sizes: DL (99 x 210 mm) and x-long (105 x 297 mm), both easy to hold in one hand, and three square sizes.

How do I divide the dishes over the four pages?

Use the two inside pages for the main choice, ideally in the order guests eat. Put drinks or desserts on the back, which guests see when the menu is closed on the table. The front stays free for your name, so the menu is recognisable at once.
