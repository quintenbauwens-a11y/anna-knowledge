Why a W Fold Leaflet Turns a Programme Into a Panorama

A Zigzag With an Extra Panel

A W fold leaflet is folded three times, back and forth, so four panels sit on each side of the sheet. That gives you eight pages. Like any concertina fold, it opens in a single pull and lays every panel out next to the others, so the whole inside reads as one long strip.

The appeal of W-fold leaflets is simple: a lot of information in a small closed size, with nothing hidden away. The reader never has to hunt for the next page, because every panel is already in view.

Where a Long Strip Helps

Some content is easier to follow in a row than on stacked pages. Festival and event programmes are the obvious example, with one panel for each day or stage. A distillery or brewery visitor centre can take guests through the four stages of production, one per panel, so the leaflet follows the tour they are on. Visitors can check where they are and what comes next.

Turn the leaflet over and there are four more panels for the practical side of an event: ticket prices, gate times and how to get there. Keep these apart from the programme, so the inside stays about what happens when.

Getting the Panels Right

Start by naming the job of each inside panel and check that the four parts are roughly the same length. When one part runs long, split it in two or move the overflow to an outside panel. The cover should say what the leaflet is about in a few words, such as the festival name and the dates, and the back is the place for your contact details and a QR code.

Fewer Panels or a Different Fold?

Three panels a side may be plenty; in that case the Z-fold leaflets give you the same zigzag with six pages. For a fold that reveals content in stages instead, look through our other folded leaflets.

Frequently Asked Questions for W-Fold Leaflets

How do the four panels open?

All together, in one pull. The three folds run in alternating directions, so the eight pages spread out side by side as soon as the cover is pulled open. Nothing is tucked inside, which keeps a long sequence, such as the stages of a distillery tour, in order.

How small can a W fold go?

The smallest folded size is A7, a tiny fold-out for a counter or ticket desk. A6 measures 105 x 148 mm when folded, DL is 99 x 210 mm, A5 is 148 x 210 mm and A4 is 210 x 297 mm, for when the panels need more room.

What paper weight works for a W fold?

Weights for this fold go up to 250 gsm on glossy paper, with 90 gsm as the lightest. Paper up to 170 gsm is delivered folded. At 250 gsm, each fold line is scored so the paper does not crack, giving you a thicker programme for the gate.

Can I have it in landscape?

There is no landscape version of this fold; you can choose portrait or square. For a day-by-day programme, choose portrait: each tall panel has room for a full day, with the times and names listed one under the other. Square panels are shorter, so they hold less text per day.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.
