Z Fold Menus: Six Panels Open Side by Side

How a Zigzag Menu Opens

Z fold menus have two parallel folds that alternate direction, so the sheet folds back and forth like an accordion. When guests pull the front panel, the menu opens out completely and all six panels line up in one row. Nothing stays hidden behind a flap, and no panel is more important than the next. Folded up, the sheet becomes a slim card with the cover on top, the side customers see first.

Compact When Closed, Complete When Open

That combination is why takeaway businesses like this fold. A burger takeaway can hand the closed menu over the counter or drop it into a bag with an order, and at home it opens into the full menu in one go: burgers, sides and shakes side by side. A Thai delivery menu benefits too, as a long list of curries and noodle dishes reads easily when it is spread across a row of panels.

Because each panel sits next to the others, it helps to group dishes per panel, with a clear heading on each. Customers can then scan along the row and stop at the section they want.

A Drinks List That Reads Left to Right

A coffee bar can lay out its drinks across the panels, with espresso drinks and teas each in their own column. Customers read the list left to right while they queue, and the closed menu takes up little room on the counter between orders. Seasonal drinks can take the last panel, so regulars spot what is new without searching.

Takeaway Speed or a Flap That Opens First?

Both have six panels. With tri-fold menus, the outer panels are folded inwards and guests open a flap before they see the inside, which is useful when one section should come first. The zigzag shows everything at once, so choose it when speed matters more than surprise, as it does for a takeaway or a drinks list read in a queue. Frequently Asked Questions for Z Fold Menus

What should the closed front of a takeaway menu show?

The closed front is what customers see on the counter and in the bag, so give it your name and phone number and say how to order. A strong photo of your best-selling dish helps people recognise the menu when they want to order again.

Which sizes are offered?

Five portrait sizes are offered: DL (99 x 210 mm), A6 (105 x 148 mm), A5 (148 x 210 mm), A4 (210 x 297 mm) and 1/3 A3 (297 x 140 mm); square comes in three sizes. Sizes are measured on the folded menu. A5 and DL keep a takeaway menu compact.

Can I choose landscape or square for a zigzag menu?

Yes, the zigzag comes in landscape and square as well as portrait. Portrait makes every panel a tall column, landscape gives wider panels with room for photos, and square panels create an even row when the menu is fully opened.

Which paper weight should I use for menus handed out with orders?

Light weights such as 115 gsm or 130 gsm keep each copy light and take up little space in a bag. Counter menus that stay put can go up to 250 gsm or 300 gsm for a firmer feel.
