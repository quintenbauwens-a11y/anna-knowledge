What an Extra Panel Does for a Roll Fold Leaflet

Eight Pages, Rolled Up Neatly

Take one long sheet, fold it three times and roll each panel in over the next: that is how roll fold leaflets are made. The result has four panels on each side, eight pages in total, yet it closes down to the size of a single panel. It opens step by step, not all at once, so the reader is led through your content in the order you choose.

Once a six-page leaflet can no longer hold everything, this is the tidy answer: you keep a compact leaflet and simply gain a fourth panel on each side.

Writing for a Roll Fold

Think of the leaflet as a short guided tour. The cover names the place or the offer, the first panel to unroll gives the welcome, the middle panels cover the main points, and the last inside panel ends with the next step. Write the panel headings first and fill in the text afterwards; it keeps each part short and stops one panel from swallowing the rest.

What to Do With the Extra Room

The fourth panel changes what you can fit in without crowding. Some typical ways to use it:

  • a service guide for a county council, with one department per panel
  • a members' guide for a golf club, covering the course, the clubhouse, competitions and fees
  • a season brochure for an arts centre, with shows grouped by month

Treat each panel as one chapter of the story you are telling. Short headings and one image per panel keep a long leaflet easy to scan.

The leaflet comes in two shapes. Portrait gives the council service guide a tall panel for each department. In square, the arts centre brochure can give every show an image of the same size.

If three panels a side will do, the six-page trifold leaflets keep things lighter and simpler. You can compare every option in our folded leaflet range before you decide.

Frequently Asked Questions for Roll Fold Leaflets

When does a roll fold need four panels instead of three?

Count the parts of your content. With a welcome plus four topics, such as a council's services or the areas of a golf club, three panels a side force two topics to share. A fourth panel gives each topic its own space, and the leaflet still closes small.

Which closed sizes can I order?

DL, folded from a 391 x 210 mm sheet, gives a slim leaflet for a tourist office rack. A4, from 831 x 297 mm, leaves room for a members' guide with photos on every panel. A5 (583 x 210 mm), A6, A7 and three square sizes complete the range.

Which paper should I pick for eight pages?

With four layers in one closed leaflet, lighter paper keeps it slim, and anything between 90 and 170 gsm arrives folded. For a more solid feel, glossy paper goes to 250 or 300 gsm; those heavier weights get a pressed crease at each fold, which stops the paper cracking.

Can the inside work as one long spread?

Yes. Once fully opened, the four inside panels sit next to each other, so a timeline, map or product photo can run across them. Keep headings and faces clear of the three folds, and check that each panel still makes sense when it is seen on its own.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.
