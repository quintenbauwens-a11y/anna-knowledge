Forex Menus: A Rigid Board in Place of a Paper Card

What Changes When the Menu Is a Board

With forex menus, the design is printed straight onto a rigid PVC foam board rather than onto paper. There is nothing to fold or open: the whole menu is one stiff panel, and the layout you design is exactly what guests see, with no crease running through a photo or a price. Guests take it in with a single look. It stays flat in the hand even when it is passed along a queue or across a table.

Snack Bar Counters and Festival Stands

At a snack bar counter, customers order quickly and often while standing. A board menu at the counter or in their hands gives them a clear overview of the chips, burgers and drinks without any pages to manage. Staff can hand it over, take it back and give it to the next customer in line.

A festival stand faces the same rush on a bigger scale. Queues are long and decisions are quick. A rigid menu passed down the line helps people know what they want before they reach the front.

A Fixed Drinks Menu by the Pool

A poolside bar usually serves the same drinks and snacks all season. Because a board is printed once and then used as it is, forex makes the most sense for that kind of fixed menu. Such a list is usually short, so one board holds it with room for clear headings and prices that are easy to read. Guests walking up from the pool can read the board at the bar in a few seconds and order straight away.

Paper Card or Board?

A menu that changes every day is easier to keep up to date on paper, and flat menus on a single card are the simplest way to do that. A board fits a short, fixed menu that guests handle again and again. For every menu format in one place, browse menu cards.

Frequently Asked Questions for Forex Menus

Can a board menu be printed on both sides?

Yes. Double-sided printing gives the board a second face, for example the everyday snack list on the front and the weekend specials on the back. Single-sided printing is the simpler option when the menu fits on one face and the back can stay plain.

What exactly is forex board?

Made of PVC foam, forex is a rigid board. Printed on it, your menu becomes one stiff panel instead of a sheet of paper or card. There is no fold and no cover to design around, only the menu itself on a single board.

What sizes can I order?

Three sizes are available: A5, A4 and A3. A5 is the most compact and easy to pass along a queue, A4 gives room for a standard menu, and A3 is the largest board for a full drinks list or a menu with many items.

File guidelines

Add 5 mm bleed.

To allow leeway for cutting differences in production, always add a bleed of 5 mm around all edges of your design. Make sure colours, background pictures and layouts extend into the bleed area so as to avoid leaving white lines as a result of cutting tolerances. You can submit your file with crop marks and printer's marks.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 150 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 150 dpi.

Keep 10 mm safety margin.

Keep all important text and images at least 10 mm away from the edge of the finished size.
