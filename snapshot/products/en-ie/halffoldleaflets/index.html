When a Half Fold Leaflet Is All You Need

Four Pages That Open in One Movement

A half fold leaflet is a single sheet with one fold through the centre, so it opens like a card, with the front cover first, two facing pages inside and the back page last. Nothing is tucked behind a flap and nobody has to work out which way to unfold it. For a lot of businesses and clubs, that is exactly the right amount of leaflet: enough space to explain, little enough to be read in full.

A plain structure also makes the design easier to plan. You write one headline for the front, one message across the middle and the practical details on the back. If you find yourself cutting text to make it fit, the content probably needs a bigger size rather than a second fold.

Typical Jobs for Four Pages

Four pages are made for short, focused content:

  • a treatment and fee list for a physio clinic
  • a parish or community centre newsletter with dates and notices
  • a welcome leaflet with house information for a B&B or guesthouse

In each case, the cover tells people what the leaflet is about at a glance and the spread inside does the explaining.

What Each Shape Does

A physio clinic's treatment and fee list sits well in portrait, with one treatment under the next. A B&B welcome leaflet can go landscape, so the inside spread takes a wide photo of the house. A community centre newsletter works in square, with the dates on one inside page and the notices on the other.

Folds With More Room

When four pages are not enough, look at tri-fold leaflets, which add a third panel on each side, or at cross fold leaflets when you need room for an event site plan. You can also browse all folded leaflets to see the different folds side by side. If your message fits on four pages, though, one fold keeps it short, clear and easy to read.

Frequently Asked Questions for Half Fold Leaflets

Should I choose a flat leaflet or a folded one?

Choose a flat leaflet when your message is one offer or one event that reads at a glance. Go for half fold leaflets when you need a cover plus room inside for detail, such as a list of services, a price list or the full schedule for a day.

Can a half fold work as a small brochure?

Yes. Four pages give you a compact brochure for one product, one property or one service: a photo and title on the cover, features and benefits across the inside, and contact details on the back. For a longer catalogue, a fold with more panels is the better route.

What is the DL option?

The DL option starts as a 198 x 210 mm sheet and folds in half to DL, a tall and narrow format. You get four slim pages that keep a list of services or a short programme easy to scan, and a closed leaflet that takes little counter space.

Which paper weight should I pick for leaflets people keep?

A price list on a reception desk and a club handout in a kit bag need different paper. For the desk, 250 gsm or more gives a thicker sheet; glossy goes up to 400 gsm. For the kit bag or a door drop, 115 or 135 gsm is lighter.

Are heavier leaflets folded before delivery?

If your parish newsletter or club handout is printed on 170 gsm or lighter, it is delivered folded. Choose 250 gsm or heavier and the leaflets are creased instead, with a scored line along the centre so the paper does not crack at the fold.

How many pages do I need to design?

You design four pages, set out as two sides of one sheet. The outside carries the back page and the front cover next to each other, and the inside holds the two facing pages people see when they open the leaflet. Keep important text clear of the centre fold.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure no elements in your artwork are set on overprint unless you want this effect in your design. You can read more about overprint in our Help Centre.
