Why a Trifold Brochure Reads in the Right Order

Two Folds, Six Panels

Fold a sheet twice, roll both outer panels inwards, and you have a trifold brochure: six panels, three on each side, that close like a wallet. You will find it in tourist offices and waiting rooms, because it holds a fair amount of information yet closes into a slim, tidy leaflet.

A Reading Order You Control

Trifold leaflets reveal their content in stages, so think of the three inside panels as the destination. The detail, prices or map sit there, and the other pages lead up to it. The cover only has to say who you are. The panel that folds in is seen next, so use it for one hook, such as the highlight a visitor should not miss. The back is a calm place for contact details, directions and opening hours.

Working this way keeps each panel short. If one panel is crammed while another is half empty, move content along the sequence rather than shrinking the type.

Picking a Shape

Trifold leaflets come in portrait, landscape and square. Portrait gives the classic tall panels that most people expect. Landscape makes each panel wide and low, handy for photos of rooms, views or products. Square creates three even frames that look modern on a counter and leave plenty of space around a logo.

Good Jobs for a Trifold

Businesses and organisations use the fold for information people take away and read later:

  • a visitor guide for a heritage site, with the route and the highlights on the inside panels
  • a membership pack for a GAA club with fees, training times and club contacts
  • a services guide for a local dental or GP practice with treatments and the team

Frequently Asked Questions for Trifold Leaflets

Which sheet is a DL tri fold made from?

An A4 sheet, folded twice, gives a DL tri fold with six tall, narrow panels. Closed, it is the slim DL format people know from leaflet racks, a good choice when your content is mostly text, such as a list of treatments or a short visitor guide.

Can I get an A4 or A3 trifold?

Yes. For an A4 leaflet when closed, the sheet is 630 x 297 mm before it is folded twice. An A3 sheet folds to one third of A3, a tall shape with large panels. For counters, there is also A5, folded from a 441 x 210 mm sheet, and A6.

How is a rolled-in fold different from a zigzag?

In a rolled-in fold, both outer panels tuck inside, so the leaflet opens in stages and the cover protects the rest. A zigzag fold alternates direction and opens in one pull, showing all panels at once. Choose the rolled version for a reveal, the zigzag for a sequence.

Which paper weights can I choose?

On glossy paper, the lightest weight is 90 gsm and the heaviest 400 gsm. Anything up to 170 gsm is delivered folded and keeps the leaflet light for mailings. Weights from 250 gsm are creased so the folds stay clean, which gives a brochure that feels more substantial.

What should go on the front cover?

Keep the front cover simple: your name or logo, one headline that says what the leaflet is about and, if you have one, a strong photo. Leave prices and detail for inside. A clear cover helps people pick the right leaflet from a busy rack or reception desk.

File guidelines

Add 3 mm bleed.

To allow leeway for cutting differences in production, always add a bleed of 3 mm around all edges of your design. Make sure colours, background pictures and layouts extend into the bleed area so as to avoid leaving white lines as a result of cutting tolerances.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

Keep all important text and images at least 4 mm away from the edge of the finished size.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

Save your files in an editable, print-ready file formats (PDF (1.4 or higher), TIFF, EPS, JPG). Deliver your files in one layer: the background layer.

Convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts.

Make sure you convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts when saving it to PDF.

Fonts should be at least 6pt.

Fonts should be at least 6 pt.

Use the right values for full colour black.

Use Rich Black (C:63% M:52% Y:51% K:100%) for larger areas and text to get a deep black colour. Do not use this value for text smaller than 15 pt or plain text. This can only be used in full colour printing. For designing black smaller texts use 100% black.

Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Force Colour Images to CMYK with a 260% ink limit (in Photoshop > Convert Colour Profile > Photoshop 5 Default CMYK). Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.
