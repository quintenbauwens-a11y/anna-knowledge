How a Z Fold Leaflet Lays Your Story Out Flat

One Pull and It Is Open

Z-fold leaflets are folded back and forth like a fan, with two folds running in opposite directions. Hold the cover, give it one pull, and all three panels on that side open at once. People sometimes call it a zigzag or concertina leaflet. With three panels on each side you get six pages, and none of them is hidden behind a flap.

Because readers see the panels next to each other, the leaflet behaves more like a short strip than a little book. That makes it a natural home for anything with a beginning, a middle and an end.

Planning the Panels

Planning a Z-fold leaflet starts with the sequence, so work backwards from it. Write down the three stages your reader needs on the inside, such as before, during and after, and give each one its own panel. Then decide what goes on the outside: a cover that names the topic, a panel with the basics, and the back for contact details. Photos can run across the inside as one wide image if you keep faces and headlines away from the two folds. You can order it in portrait, landscape or square, on glossy, matte, uncoated, recycled or natural-look paper.

Where a Zigzag Helps

A school can walk parents through enrolment in three steps: the application, the confirmation and the first day. A parish or local history group can lay out a heritage timeline with one era per panel, so readers move through the centuries in order. Estate agents and builders can show a new development with a site plan across the open inside, so people see the location and the homes in one view.

Six Pages, or More?

If you would rather reveal your content in stages, a rolled-in trifold leaflet does that. For a longer sequence, the W-fold leaflets give you four panels a side, and the full set of all folded leaflets is there to compare.

Frequently Asked Questions for Z-Fold Leaflets

Which sizes does a zigzag leaflet fold down to?

A zigzag leaflet folds down to A4, A5, A6, DL, one third of A3 or a square size. The tall third-of-A3 format gives each stage of a heritage timeline a long panel. A4 starts as a 630 x 297 mm sheet and A5 as 444 x 210 mm.

Is a z fold brochure better than a rolled-in fold?

Neither is better in general; they do different jobs. The zigzag version of the brochure opens in one pull and shows every panel together, which helps with steps, routes and timelines. A rolled-in fold opens in stages, which works for a reveal or a cover that protects the inside.

Does the zigzag fold come in square?

Yes, square is offered alongside portrait and landscape. A square leaflet opens into three even panels in a row, a tidy frame for product photos or a short gallery. Landscape gives wide, low panels, handy for a route drawn across the whole spread.

How heavy can the paper be?

The glossy range runs between 90 and 400 gsm. Weights up to 170 gsm are delivered folded, a good fit for leaflet drops. From 250 gsm, both fold lines are scored first so the thicker sheet bends without cracking, giving a visitor centre or trade stand a more substantial leaflet.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.
