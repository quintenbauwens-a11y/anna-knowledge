French Fold Leaflets With Room for a Full Map

Folded Twice, Opened Wide

A French fold leaflet is folded down the middle and then across, so the two folds meet at right angles. Closed, cross fold leaflets are small and tidy. Opened, they turn into one sheet four times the folded size, with eight pages to design.

That change of scale is what makes the format useful. A visitor can carry it in a coat pocket around a showground or town, then open it out whenever they need the full picture.

Because the sheet is printed in one piece, a map or plan runs without breaks between pages, which keeps routes and floor plans easy to read.

Maps, Plans and Posters

Most cross fold jobs come down to one large image or plan:

  • a site map for an agricultural show or ploughing match, with stands, rings and car parks marked
  • a county walking-trail network for a tourism office, with every route on one sheet
  • a campus or hospital site map that shows visitors each building and entrance

A Simple Way to Plan the Artwork

Lay out the full open sheet first, then mark where the two folds will fall. Keep labels, names and arrows away from the centre point where the folds cross. Once the inside works, design the outer pages around it: a cover with a clear title, a back page with contact details and a legend or short guide on the pages in between. Glossy, matte, uncoated and recycled papers are available, on a portrait or square sheet.

Fewer Folds, Fewer Pages

If you only need four pages that read one after the other, with no large sheet, a half fold leaflet is the simpler choice. When your content is a sequence of steps rather than one large image, Z-fold leaflets lay the panels out in a row, so each step gets its own panel. Frequently Asked Questions for Cross Fold Leaflets

Is a French fold the same as a cross fold?

Yes. French fold, cross fold and map fold all describe a sheet folded in half and then in half again across the first fold. The result has eight pages and opens out to four times its folded size, ready for a map or a poster.

How large is the sheet once unfolded?

The open sheet is A2 for an A4 leaflet, A3 for an A5 leaflet and A4 for an A6 leaflet. A2 gives the most space for a detailed event map, while A6, folded from A4, is a compact option for a simple plan or a short trail.

Does a French fold brochure come folded?

Yes. This fold is printed on paper up to 170 gsm, with 90, 115, 135 and 170 gsm on glossy, and paper in that range is delivered folded. Lighter weights keep the folded leaflet thin, which helps when you are handing out hundreds at an event entrance.

Which shapes does the French fold come in?

Portrait and square are offered; landscape is not available for this fold. A portrait sheet opens tall, which works for a route or poster, and a square sheet opens with equal sides, which gives a site map the same space on every side.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure no elements in your artwork are set on overprint unless you want this effect in your design. You can read more about overprint in our Help Centre.
