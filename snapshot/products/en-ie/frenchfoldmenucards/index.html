Why a Cross Fold Menu Works Best as One Open Sheet

Small When Closed, Generous When Open

Folded in half and then again across the first fold, at a right angle, a cross fold menu closes into a small, tidy card. Guests unfold it into one big sheet rather than turning pages. Everything you print inside is visible at the same time, like a map of your menu. Guests open it in two steps, one fold at a time, until the whole sheet is in view.

A Gin or Whisky List at a Glance

That single surface is what makes comparing easy. With cross fold menus, a gin or whisky list can show every bottle on one open sheet, grouped by region or flavour, with tasting notes next to each. Guests scan the whole range and spot the one they want, instead of flicking back and forth between pages, and staff can talk them through a choice with everything in view. Bars with a long range can add a short introduction on the outside, so guests know what to expect before they open the card.

Event Catering and Chef's Menus

Caterers often need a menu that is easy to set out at each place and still shows a complete evening. For event catering, the closed card sits on the plate or napkin, and the open sheet presents every course, the drinks and perhaps a short welcome. Printed on uncoated paper, the card can also take a guest's name written by hand. A chef's menu works in the same spirit: a sequence of courses that guests can follow as one story when they open the card.

Cross Fold or Half Fold?

If your menu reads better as pages to turn, half fold menus with one fold and a cover are the simpler route. The cross fold is the stronger choice when the content belongs together on one surface, and once closed again they take up little room on a crowded table. Frequently Asked Questions for Cross Fold Menus

How should I lay out artwork for a menu folded twice?

Start with the inside as one large page and leave a little space along both fold lines, so no text sits on a crease. On the outside, reserve the panel guests see first for the event name or your logo, as that is how the closed card is recognised.

What sizes can I pick?

Choose A4 (210 x 297 mm) for a long list of drinks or courses, A5 (148 x 210 mm) for most menus or A6 (105 x 148 mm) for a small card at each place setting; square adds three more sizes. These are the sizes of the menu when it is folded.

Which papers and weights are offered?

You can choose between glossy, matte, uncoated and recycled papers; weights start at 80 gsm on recycled paper and go up to 170 gsm. Glossy paper gives photos of drinks a bright finish, while recycled paper gives the card a more natural look.

Can I order a square version?

Yes, square and portrait are both offered. Square opens into a balanced sheet that works well for a grid of drinks, while portrait gives a tall layout for courses read in order.
