File guidelines

For preparing your file we recommend to use our template.

For preparing your file we recommend using our template. This is the best way to deliver your file perfectly.

Add 5 mm bleed.

To allow leeway for cutting differences in production, always add a bleed of 5 mm around all edges of your design. Make sure colours, background pictures and layouts extend into the bleed area so as to avoid leaving white lines as a result of cutting tolerances. You can submit your file with crop marks and printer's marks.

Keep 10 mm safety margin.

Keep all important text and images at least 10 mm away from the edge of the finished size.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

Save your files in an editable, print-ready file formats (PDF (1.4 or higher), TIFF, EPS, JPG). Deliver your files in one layer: the background layer.

Here you can find the customized submission specifications for contour cutting.

If you want your product to be cut to a custom shape, you will have to add it as a vector shape on top of your design. Make sure the width of the stroke is 1 pt, the colour is a 100% magenta spot and named "cutcontour". You only need to add one contour line. If you want us to adjust it for you, please send the custom shape on a separate page in the desired position.

Please note that bleed is still needed for custom shaped designs, meaning your design must go at least 5 mm past the contour cut line.

Convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts.

Make sure you convert fonts to outlines or embed all the fonts when saving it to PDF.

Fonts should be at least 12 pt.

Fonts should be at least 12 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure no elements in your artwork are set on overprint unless you want this effect in your design. You can read more about overprint in our Help Centre.

Questions on the Aluminium Prints product page

What is this product? And what can I use it for?

A photograph on aluminium, the words say it all. A photo of your choice printed on the aluminium plate. This product is very popular as a decoration and even marketing materials.

What materials can I choose from?

White aluminium: Light weight, consists of two aluminium plates with an HPL core (Dibond). It does not bend and is waterproof. Also, it has a naturally distinctive look. All colours except white can be printed, giving it a unique result.

Brushed aluminium: The brushed aluminium plate is silver / gray in color, in which the structure of the brushed aluminium is visible. Your picture will get unique and raw look through the aluminium texture.

Does Aluminium Prints come with a suspension/hanging set?

Aluminium Prints do have the option for suspension/hanging sets, or none at all of course. If you have opted for the larger print sizes and require a hanging set, then we recommend choosing the biggest hanging set to ensure stability.

Can Aluminium Prints be used outside?

All photographs on aluminium are suitable for usage indoor and outdoor.

Can Aluminium Prints be used as a promotional product?

Yes, photo on aluminium can be used as a promotional product. We also recommend our Aluminium Business Signsif you are looking for a specific size.

Print deviation

Printing Aluminium Prints is different from printing paper, not a single Aluminium Prints is the same. There can be a colour deviation when printing photos which can result in the final result being printed darker than expected.

Which production techniques do you use?

For the production of Aluminium Prints, we use the following technique(s):

UV-print: The product is printed with eco-friendly UV ink, which is immediately cured with a UV light. Curing ensures that the wet ink doesn't spread once printed, offering sharper quality prints.
