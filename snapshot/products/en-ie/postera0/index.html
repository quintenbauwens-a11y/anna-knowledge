Making a Big Impression with an A0 Poster

Some messages have to work before anyone gets close. An A0 poster, at 841 x 1189 mm, tops the A series, and that's the job A0 poster printing does best: one image and a few words that carry across a hotel lobby or the back of a stand at the RDS.

Seen from Across the Room

A0 is double an A1 and four times an A2, which is why it's the size to choose when being seen from afar counts for more than the fine print. Think of the poster that tells conference guests which room they're in, or the one sheet behind a sales counter that names the brand. If your poster is mainly read at arm's length, though, A1 posters are often the better choice, with the same proportions at half the size. At a trade fair or an agricultural show, the first few seconds decide if someone walks over, and one large, simple sheet makes those seconds count.

Upright or Wide

Hung upright, an A0 has the height for one tall image with the headline above it, the way most posters at a show are read. Turn it on its side and you get 1189 mm of width for a floor plan, a timeline or a row of product photos. Either way, keep the layout simple: one message large enough to read from the door, with the detail lower down for anyone who walks over.

One Big Sheet Among Smaller Ones

At an event an A0 rarely works alone. It carries the name and the main message, while smaller posters near the entrance or on tables carry the detail. Every A size is a scaled version of the next, so one design can be adapted to each of them without starting again. On the big sheet itself, the paper really shows: gloss for vivid colour, silk when the words have to stay clear under strong lighting. It often helps to settle the A0 design first and build the smaller sizes from it, so the big sheet sets the colours and the tone for the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions for A0 Posters

What are the exact dimensions of A0?

A full 841 x 1189 mm. Your poster is printed at exactly that size, upright or turned to landscape, so set up the artwork at full A0 from the start and you won't need to resize anything later.

Where can I get A0 printed?

HelloPrint prints them at full A0 size: you pick the paper, a lamination if you want one, and portrait or landscape. The paper is the main decision, since it shapes both the look of the poster and how well it travels.

Can my A1 artwork be enlarged to A0?

Yes. A0 is exactly double A1 and keeps the same proportions, so the layout carries over untouched. Check that small text still looks balanced at full size: a caption that worked on an A1 can look lost on a sheet of 841 x 1189 mm.

Which finish works best on a photo-heavy A0 design?

Gloss lamination, which is recommended for photos: the thin glossy film deepens colours and gives the whole sheet a shiny surface. Another route is printing on 190 gsm photo paper, whose smooth coating brings out fine detail and deep colours in every image.

Is matt lamination better for an A0 with lots of text?

Usually, yes. Matt lamination is a thin, non-reflective film that keeps text easy to read, and it is made for text-heavy or minimalist designs. On a big sheet under bright hall lights, less glare lets people read it from more angles.

Which A0 paper copes best with being moved between events?

The 250 gsm papers, gloss or silk, both thick, card-like, sturdy and non-bending. Add a lamination for a film that protects against tearing, and your A0 poster is far better prepared for repeated trips to shows and conferences.

Is A0 too big for an office or classroom wall?

Not if the wall has room to spare: an A0 becomes one centrepiece the whole room can see, like a project timeline or a large map. For a noticeboard, or a poster people read from their desks, A1 is half the size with the same proportions.

Is there a minimum quantity for A0?

No, you can start at one. That makes A0 posters easy to order as the one big sheet at the back of a stand or on the end wall of a function room, and you can choose a larger quantity when every venue needs its own copy.

File guidelines

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

You can download our guides and templates in the template section. Remember to delete the template layers before exporting your design to PDF.

If you already have a design, no worries; we accept other file formats, including JPEG, PNG, or EPS.

For photographic content, high-resolution JPEGs are recommended, preferably without compression. For graphics or text-based content, we recommend print-ready, vector-based PDFs. For content that is a combination of graphics and photography, we suggest using print-ready PDFs to ensure the best quality.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure no elements in your artwork are set on overprint unless you want this effect in your design. You can read more about overprint in our Help Centre.

Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Force Colour Images to CMYK with a 260% ink limit (in Photoshop > Convert Colour Profile > Photoshop 5 Default CMYK). Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Using a border/frame is not recommended

It is recommended to count with a margin of error when stacks of copies are trimmed during the production process. This slight cutting deviation is usually not noticeable, nevertheless, we recommend avoiding adding borders smaller than 20 mm in your design for any product smaller than an A4 (210 x 297 mm). Otherwise, the design might end up looking off-centre.
