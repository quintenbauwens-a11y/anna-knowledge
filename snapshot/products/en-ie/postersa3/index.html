What an A3 Poster Says at Arm's Length

Not every message needs a whole wall. Measuring 297 x 420 mm, an A3 poster is made for places people pass at arm's length, like the parish hall noticeboard or the back of a door, and that is where an A3 does its job without taking over the space.

Close-Up Messages That Change Often

A3 notices tend to have a short life and a clear purpose, like this week's opening hours or the date of the next committee meeting. Because each one is read by people standing right in front of it, a few well-chosen lines do more than a crowded sheet. That makes the size easy to work with. For a shop or a school that needs the same notice in several places, a small run of identical A3 sheets covers every door and board at once. Ordering a few identical sheets also means a spare is ready when a notice gets torn or taken down by mistake. Keep the most important line at the top in large letters, so people can take it in as they pass, and put the details below for anyone who stops.

A3 or Something Bigger?

Up close is where A3 is at its strongest. Once your message has to reach people on the far side of a room, it's worth stepping up to the A2 poster size, and because A sizes are scaled versions of each other, your layout moves across without a redesign. Many organisers use both: one larger poster where people gather, and A3 copies along the corridors.

Paper for a Sheet Seen Up Close

If your A3 is mostly words, a silk sheet takes the glare off so every line stays clear. If it's a photo or a bold graphic, gloss makes the colours vivid. For notices that go up and come down every week, a lighter paper keeps a regular run economical, while a heavier sheet is worth it for a sign that stays up all year.

Frequently Asked Questions for A3 Posters

What is the A3 poster size in millimetres?

297 x 420 mm, the only size this page prints, upright or turned to landscape. A document you already have at A4 scales up without any cropping, because every A size keeps the same proportions.

Can I print an A3 poster in landscape?

Yes. Turned on its side, the sheet is 420 mm wide and 297 mm tall, which gives a row of photos, a short timetable or a wide headline more room. Portrait remains the usual choice on a door or a narrow noticeboard.

When does double-sided printing make sense for A3?

When the poster hangs where people pass on both sides, like a shop door or a window facing the street. Double-sided printing covers front and back, while single-sided keeps costs down for anything pinned flat against a board.

Is photo paper a good choice for a small A3 print?

Yes, especially when the picture is the whole point of the poster. Photo paper at 190 gsm is smooth and coated for sharp detail and delivers deep colours and a high-quality look, which shows on a product photo in a shop window.

Can I write on an A3 poster?

Yes, on 120 gsm offset or 250 gsm offset. Both are uncoated, naturally matt and easy to write on, so you can print A3 posters as rotas, class lists or notices you fill in by hand.

Which A3 paper holds its shape on a door?

The 250 gsm papers. 250 gsm gloss and 250 gsm silk are thick, card-like and non-bending, and 250 gsm offset is sturdy too, so an A3 poster on a door that opens and closes all day stays flat.

Can an A3 notice go up outside?

Yes, on 170 gsm PVC waterproof, a plastic poster material that resists water and is made for outdoor use, so the opening hours on a gate or the notice at the pitch entrance stay readable after a shower.

What's the smallest number of A3 posters I can order?

One is the minimum, so a single A3 for the staffroom or the till is fine. A3 notices tend to change often, so you can order just what this week's message needs and update it next time.

File guidelines

Save your files in uneditable, print-ready file formats.

You can download our guides and templates in the template section. Remember to delete the template layers before exporting your design to PDF.

If you already have a design, no worries; we accept other file formats, including JPEG, PNG, or EPS.

For photographic content, high-resolution JPEGs are recommended, preferably without compression. For graphics or text-based content, we recommend print-ready, vector-based PDFs. For content that is a combination of graphics and photography, we suggest using print-ready PDFs to ensure the best quality.

Use CMYK as the colour mode.

Prepare your files using CMYK as the colour mode, not RGB. Use colourmode FOGRA39 (ISO Coated v2). If you are unsure of the colour mode of your file, don't worry we can always convert this for you. Keep in mind this can cause the colours to differ from the original file.

Resolution of at least 300 dpi.

Make sure the resolution of the images and graphics in your artwork are at least 300 dpi.

Keep 4 mm safety margin

During production, there might be a slight cut deviation. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

  • Add a 3 mm bleed around all edges of your design.
  • Keep important elements 4 mm away from the edge or folding lines.
  • Avoid using borders or frames on smaller formats like labels or business cards.

For this product, the minimum font size is 6 pt. To avoid any issues, we recommend the following:

Note that we don't check spelling or typographical errors.

Check line thickness.

Lines should be at least 0.25 pt. Negative lines should be at least 0.5 pt.

Check your artwork on overprint.

Make sure no elements in your artwork are set on overprint unless you want this effect in your design. You can read more about overprint in our Help Centre.

Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Force Colour Images to CMYK with a 260% ink limit (in Photoshop > Convert Colour Profile > Photoshop 5 Default CMYK). Make sure the ink coverage isn't higher than 300%.

Using a border/frame is not recommended

It is recommended to count with a margin of error when stacks of copies are trimmed during the production process. This slight cutting deviation is usually not noticeable, nevertheless, we recommend avoiding adding borders smaller than 20 mm in your design for any product smaller than an A4 (210 x 297 mm). Otherwise, the design might end up looking off-centre.
