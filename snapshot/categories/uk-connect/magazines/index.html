Print your own magazine

Want to print your own magazine? Start by defining your audience and deciding what you want the publication to achieve.

Choose the right magazine binding

The binding has an important impact on the appearance and functionality of your magazine.

Stapled magazines are a popular choice for thinner publications. The pages are folded and secured with staples through the spine. This is a practical option for company magazines, event publications, newsletters and association magazines.

Perfect-bound magazines are suited to thicker publications. The pages are glued into the spine, creating a clean and professional finish. This type of binding works particularly well for lifestyle magazines, premium publications and longer editorial projects.

Wire-bound magazines are practical when you want the pages to open easily or lie flat. This can be useful for guides, workbooks and publications that need to be consulted regularly.

Choose the right paper

Paper plays a major role in the appearance and feel of a printed magazine.

Gloss paper can work particularly well for photography-led publications, as it can bring out colourful images and graphic elements. Matt paper creates a more understated and editorial look and can be a good choice for magazines with a lot of written content.

You can also choose a heavier stock for the cover. A more substantial cover protects the pages inside and gives the publication a stronger presence.

Think about both the content and the intended reading experience when choosing your paper.

Frequently Asked Questions: Magazine printing

What paper types can I choose for magazine printing?

When you print magazines with us, you can choose from several paper weights depending on how you want your printed magazine to look and feel:

90 gsm: Our lightest and most affordable option. Ideal for cheap magazine printing and large distribution runs.

110–120 gsm: Slightly thicker and more durable, often chosen when ordering magazines online for everyday use.

135 gsm: A popular choice for business magazines, offering a more premium feel while remaining flexible.

170 gsm: Gives magazines a sturdier, professional finish, suitable for events or presentations.

250 gsm: Mainly used for covers, adding firmness and protection to your printed magazine.

What is lamination and do I need it on my cover?

Lamination adds a thin plastic layer to the cover of your printed magazines. It helps protect the cover from marks, creases and colour fading, while giving your magazine a more durable, professional finish. It’s a smart choice if your magazines will be handled often, posted out or used at events. Without lamination, the cover is more likely to scuff during delivery or distribution. When laminated magazines are packed together, there’s no risk of ink transferring between covers.

Note: Laminated paper products are water-resistant, not waterproof.

Can I select any paper type or weight for lamination?

No, not all paper can be laminated. Lamination is only possible for paper with a Glossy or Matte cover of 250 gsm or higher.

Is the amount of pages equal to the amount of paper sheets?

No. This is because the pages are folded and printed on both sides. A sheet of paper gets folded in the middle so it covers 4 pages (2 on the front and 2 on the back). For instance, when you choose an A4 size brochure with 8 pages, we print two A3 sheets (two times as big as A4) and fold them in half, which results in a total of 8 pages.

Which magazine format should I print?

A4 (210 x 297 mm): The most common format for magazine printing. It offers plenty of space for content and visuals, making it ideal for clear layouts and comfortable reading.

A5 (148 x 210 mm): A more compact option than A4, often chosen for smaller publications such as newsletters or niche magazines. Easy to handle and a good choice for cost-effective magazine printing.

Square format: Custom magazines in a square format (for example, 210 x 210 mm) are increasingly popular thanks to their modern and distinctive look. They work especially well for photography, design or art magazines.

Other magazine formats: If you want something more original, formats like DL, A6 or landscape A5 can help your magazine stand out from the rest.
